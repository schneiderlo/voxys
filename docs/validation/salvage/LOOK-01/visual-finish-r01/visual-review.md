# Independent playable-scene visual review

## Native arrival

**The final native still meets the visual-composition portion of the early
LOOK-01 component.** It shows the whole authored skiff, dock and generator,
readable toy materials and a purposeful shore workshop. This is independent
agent review, not owner approval or acceptance of every LOOK prerequisite.
The browser canvas arrival and sampled short motion are reviewed below, with
their capture and temporal limits made explicit.

Reviewed the supplied [native arrival](images/native-arrival.png) at 1280×720 with vertical
FOV 78°, orbit yaw −1.4, elevation .60 and distance 12 m. Its companion observation
reports completed/presented tick 199, a valid unheld camera and the physical
boat at Y −1.53686 with speed .116275 m/s. This is a live, settled floating craft,
not the initial falling spawn or an offline stationary substitute. Exact image
and observation hashes are in [the input record](checks/visual-review-inputs.json).
No new capture or GPU work was performed for this review.

Compared with the original LEGO terrain prototype and the first rejected
native arrival, the improvements are concrete:

- The skiff is a recognizable assembly of pontoons, plates, helm, engine,
  winch and cradle. Its complete silhouette now fits inside the image.
  It is not a large raw box substituting for a vessel.
- Workshop/stairs, player/dock, generator and boat occupy distinct areas.
  The workshop and beacon establish a place to approach. The shore and sea
  still supply a middle distance and horizon.
- Cream plastic, teal structural panels, orange accents and restrained
  metal/rubber carry the textual construction-toy direction into actual play.
  Studs, panel edges, socket details and bevel highlights remain legible.
- The broad dock panels no longer show the prior diagonal shadow stipple.
  Ground/object shadows give the kit contact; submerged studs remain visible
  through the water. No obvious water/opaque ordering fault or part
  intersection is visible in this frame. The separate [shadow proof](shadows.md)
  supplies numerical evidence beyond this visual observation.
- The 400×245 native panel at (866,14) occupies upper-right sky/water. It no
  longer covers the workshop, boat, generator or player. Its 20 px text is
  readable and the observation reports no truncation. Building controls keep
  their separate established layout.

## Remaining quality limits

The robot is small at this wide arrival framing; facial, hand and material
microdetail cannot be judged here. The terrain still repeats regular studs
and broad simple coastal bands. The workshop and scenery are economical toy
shapes, and the horizon is sparse compared with the richer generated concept.
The new harbor view also shows less of the original panoramic island. These
are limits of this early composed section; it is not final AAA environment,
material richness or an entire world-art approval.

A still cannot establish shimmer/LOD stability, moving wet highlights, wakes,
ropes, swimming effects, frame pacing or camera transitions. Nor does this
review certify general transparent glass, production performance or missing
asset-pipeline prerequisites. No further camera-capacity increase or repeated
still-view grid is recommended from the native result.

## Browser and motion review

**The actual browser canvas arrival and sampled moving-camera record support
the same early visual direction.** The [first decoded canvas frame](images/browser-canvas-first.png)
closely matches the final native composition: whole skiff, separate dock/player
and generator, workshop/stairs, studded shore and turquoise water. Plastic,
metal accents and shadows retain the same visual character. The wave and boat
pose differ because simulation is live; this is visual agreement, not byte
identity. No new obvious water ordering or intersection defect is visible.

The [browser preflight](checks/browser-preflight.json) records 1280×720, the
matching actual view-projection matrix and orbit, and coherent geometry/
presentation tick 185. Its upper-right DOM HUD is 352×346.07 px at (904,24), with
scrollHeight and clientHeight both 344 and More controls closed. The world
recording omits DOM UI. These bounds support placement and no vertical overflow;
they do not replace a visual review of browser text or a composed page image.

The [12-second actual canvas recording](media/playable-motion.webm) contains
7,292,836 bytes. The [decoder report](checks/decoded-video.json) reports 336
decoded VP9 frames at 1280×720; the [capture record](checks/browser-capture.json)
declares a 30 fps capture target. Those counts do not establish gameplay frame
rate. This reviewer inspected the first decoded frame and the supplied
[four sampled motion frames](images/motion-samples.png), rather than claiming
to have watched every frame continuously.

The motion samples show changed camera viewpoints, a readable assembled boat,
dock and generator, submerged stud geometry, directional contact shadows and
consistent water/kit color relationships. They show no gross disappearance,
large geometry hole or obvious material/ordering break at those sampled
positions. The sparse wreck and repeated coastal terrain remain economical
art. Four samples cannot exclude brief popping, shimmer, interpolation errors,
frame stalls or camera obstruction between them; continuous temporal quality
and production performance remain outside this review.

The [page screenshot](images/rejected-browser-page.png) is **rejected capture
evidence**: `Page.captureScreenshot` returned black behind the DOM HUD, while
the actual canvas recording contains the scene. It is not counted as a passing
composed browser screenshot, silently repaired, or used to claim the game
rendered black. The exact media, capture and observation hashes are retained
in [the input record](checks/visual-review-inputs.json).

## Acceptance scope

No further composition/rendering correction is identified for this bounded
early native/browser visual-direction component from the supplied evidence.
The required whole skiff, dock, generator and walkable harbor context now read
as a construction-toy game, improving on the initial rejected view and moving
beyond the original landscape-only prototype. The artifacts support that
concrete improvement, with browser canvas capture substituted explicitly for
the failed composed-page artifact and motion review limited to samples.

This is independent agent review, not owner approval. The full LOOK-01 task's
asset prerequisites, browser DOM HUD visual parity, continuous temporal
assessment, final VIS-03/VIS-05/G08 quality and mandatory repository/publication
gates are not certified by this page. The remaining
quality limits above must stay visible in the final checkpoint.
